HELP & CRYPTOGRAPHY REFERENCE MANUAL

Theoretical fundamentals, algorithm specifications, mathematical models, cryptanalysis vulnerabilities, and user operational guidance.

01 What is Classical Cryptography?

Classical cryptography comprises encryption techniques developed and used prior to the mid-20th century, before electronic digital computers and information theory (Claude Shannon, 1949). These algorithms operate on character or digraph alphabets rather than binary bits.

Substitution vs. Transposition:

  • Substitution Ciphers: The positions of characters remain fixed, but their identities are replaced by other characters or symbols (e.g. Caesar, Monoalphabetic, Playfair, Hill, Vigenère, One-Time Pad).
  • Transposition Ciphers: The identities of the characters remain intact, but their positions or sequence within the text are permuted according to a geometric rule or key (e.g. Rail Fence, Columnar Transposition).

02 Algorithm Specifications & Formulas

1. Caesar: C = (P + k) mod 26. Vulnerable to exhaustive search (25 shifts) and letter frequency analysis (English 'E' is ~12.7%).
2. Monoalphabetic: Uses arbitrary permutation of 26 letters. Keyspace is 26! ≈ 4.03 × 10²⁶, but easily broken using single-letter and digraph frequencies.
3. Playfair: Encrypts digraphs on a 5×5 matrix (combining I & J). Equal letters in pairs take filler 'X'. Flattened single-letter frequency, but vulnerable to digraph frequency attacks.
4. Hill (2×2): C = K · P mod 26. Key matrix K requires gcd(det(K), 26) = 1. Invertible using K⁻¹ = det⁻¹ · adj(K) mod 26. Broken via known-plaintext attack with 2 pairs.
5. Vigenère: Polyalphabetic repeating key. Resists simple frequency analysis, but broken using Kasiski examination and Friedman's Index of Coincidence to determine key length.
6. One-Time Pad: Information-theoretically secure (proven by Shannon) if and only if the key is truly random, never reused, kept completely secret, and at least as long as the message.
7. Rail Fence: Transposition using a diagonal zigzag path across R rails. Preserves character frequencies; broken by trying small rail depths.
8. Columnar Transposition: Permutes columns according to alphabetical sorting of the keyword letters. Broken through anagramming and multiple anagram analysis.

04 Security Notice & Academic Disclaimer

CRITICAL SECURITY WARNING: Classical ciphers are strictly educational models. In modern cybersecurity, classical algorithms offer zero confidentiality against computers:

  • Any classical cipher can be broken in microseconds on a standard smartphone or laptop.
  • They lack integrity verification, authenticity, and non-repudiation.
  • DO NOT use classical ciphers to protect passwords, personal identification, financial tokens, credentials, or proprietary business data.
  • Modern security utilizes standards such as AES-256-GCM (symmetric), RSA-4096 / Ed25519 / ECDSA (asymmetric), and post-quantum lattice cryptography (ML-KEM, ML-DSA).
